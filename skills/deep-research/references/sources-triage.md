# Source triage

## Tiers

- Tier 1: official docs, specs, source code, papers, first-party data, maintainer blogs.
- Tier 2: established tech press, well-maintained tutorials, accepted answers, conference talks.
- Tier 3: personal blogs, forums, vendor hiring pages — use with caution, verify expertise.

Prefer Tier 1; treat secondary summaries as pointers to the primary, never as the citation for its claims.

## Screen then verify

1. Quick screen: currency, relevance, authority, accuracy, purpose.
2. Lateral check: leave the page and verify the author and venue externally (indexing, retractions, funding, data availability). Polished presentation is not proof.
3. Trace each claim to its owning source. If the primary cannot be located, flag the chain rather than citing the intermediary as the original.

## Recency and conflicts

- Pin the publication or verification date on every finding. For fast-moving tech prefer roughly the last 12 months as a default, but keep older foundations when nothing material changed.
- When credible sources conflict, check whether they answer the same question (population, method, timeframe, definitions), weigh study design over recency, check corrections, and report genuinely unresolved disagreement as unsettled with the reason.
